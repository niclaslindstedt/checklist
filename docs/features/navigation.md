# Getting around

`checklist` keeps your namespaces, lists, the [archive](feature:archive),
and undo/redo behind a single navigation menu. How you reach it depends on
how much room your screen has.

## The floating menu button

On phones the menu lives behind a **floating button**. Drag it and it
tracks your finger precisely, then **snaps to the nearest side of the
screen** and glides into place when you let go. It **remembers where you
left it**, so it stays on the edge you prefer across visits. Dragging it
downward will not accidentally trigger a pull-to-refresh, and when the
on-screen keyboard is up the button stays **above the
keyboard** where you can still reach it, instead of disappearing behind it.

Press the button and the menu slides in from whichever edge the button
rests on.

## Hiding the button

In the **installed app on Android or iOS**, you can turn the floating
button off entirely from the **General** settings. With it hidden, **swipe
in from the edge of the screen** to open the menu instead — a cleaner look
that trades the visible button for a gesture. This option only appears in
the installed app, where a swipe from the edge has room to work.

## Back and forward

The browser's **Back button knows where you've been**. Open one list, then
another, and Back returns you to the first — press it again and you keep
walking back through the lists, templates, archive visits, and namespaces
you opened, with Forward taking you out the same way. In the installed app
the back gesture does the same thing. The address bar never changes; only
the trail behind you does.

Only the places you *chose* to go become steps. A list that closes because
you archived or deleted it is not somewhere Back can strand you, and
opening the app never leaves an empty step behind before your first move.
Going back across a namespace switch returns you to that namespace on the
list you left it on.

## Linking to a list

The address bar always names the list you have open, so **bookmark it and
you have a way straight back in** — and the same link works in a note, a
calendar entry, or another checklist. Opening one goes to that list
directly instead of wherever you were last, including into another
namespace.

The link lives in the part of the address after `#`, which browsers never
send to a server, so nothing about which list you opened leaves your
device. It points at a list of *yours*: it opens on any device where you
use the same storage, and means nothing in someone else's app — a link you
keep, not a way to send a list to another person. If the list it names has
since been deleted, the app opens a real one instead and quietly corrects
the address.

## The pinned sidebar

On wider screens — anything at least as wide as the smallest iPad — there
is no floating button and no gesture to learn. The navigation simply
**stays open as a permanent sidebar** beside your list, docked on the same
edge the button would have used. Your lists are always one click away,
right where you can see them.
